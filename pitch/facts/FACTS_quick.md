## Trusted Lean 4 core (lean/: CryoLean/*.lean, Main.lean, Audit.lean, audit-axioms.json, lean-toolchain, lakefile.toml) at origin/main b2b80ff

CryoLean's trusted core is a Lean 4 (v4.35.0) project in lean/ with no external dependencies. It has 3,166 lines across 13 files. The 10 library modules under lean/CryoLean/ hold 2,919 of those lines and all 128 kernel-checked theorems (98 in Soundness.lean, 18 in Monotone.lean, 7 in Schedule.lean, 5 in Num.lean). The design is a proof sandwich. Checker.lean is the executable checker the compiled binary runs; its whole decision rule is a 5-line function. Spec.lean separately defines what ACCEPT and REJECT are supposed to mean, and Soundness.lean proves the two agree in both directions (verdict_accept_iff: accept ↔ AcceptSpec; verdict_reject_sound and verdict_reject_complete). There are exactly three verdicts (REQUIREMENTS_MET, DOES_NOT_MEET_REQUIREMENTS, NEED_MORE_INFORMATION). Theorems ensure that missing, estimated, untrusted or dimension-mismatched data can only lead to NEED_MORE_INFORMATION, and that every rejection rests on trusted evidence. Numbers are exact rationals (an Int over a Nat), each carrying a 7-component SI dimension vector (K, m, kg, s, A, mol, cd); the trusted code has no floating point and no Mathlib. 48 headline theorems are axiom-audited (Audit.lean, audit-axioms.json and the pytest REQUIRED list all agree on 48), and all 48 use only Lean's three standard axioms: 24 need only propext, 5 use Classical.choice, none uses sorryAx. A search of the code finds zero sorry, admit, native_decide or axiom declarations. The newest module, Monotone.lean, proves information monotonicity: adding or improving records can never flip a decided verdict, only resolve NEED_MORE_INFORMATION. Theorems also stop the untrusted proposers from deciding anything: the verdicts reported for Z3 or AI candidates are Lean's own, schedules can only contain experiments Lean accepted, and the industry label cannot change a verdict. An outside 'forensics' audit of the theorem statements found one real soundness gap (an untrusted 'not installed' record could force a REJECT); it was fixed and is now ruled out by theorem. Docs to correct: README.md:23 and VERIFICATION_SCOPE.md:17 still say '44 audited theorems', and the paper says '2,578 lines in nine files' and '110 theorems'. All of these predate Monotone.lean (commit 1b2c2dd); the code-derived 48, 128 and 2,919 are current, and the forensics count of 839 declarations also predates Monotone.lean. The project states its own limits: verdict_deterministic is only congruence (same inputs, same output), validSchedule_sound/_complete mostly restate a Boolean conjunction, 'AI cannot bypass the gate' holds by construction rather than by a theorem, and the proofs say nothing about whether the source data is true.

## Forall forensics: auditing the verifier itself

CryoLean ran the external for-all-dev/forensics toolkit (pinned at commit e5b80ca2) against its own trusted Lean kernel. The reason: a green `lake build` only shows that the kernel accepted the proofs, not that the theorems say what the team claims. The static extractor audited 839 elaborated declarations: 337 defs, 276 theorems (110 hand-written, 166 generated by Lean), 114 constructors, 83 instances, 15 inductives and 14 structures. It found 0 that depend on sorry, native_decide or a non-standard axiom, 0 statement smells and 0 unsafe flags. It got there after one fix: a `partial` JSON decoder was made total. Next, seven LLM-judge indicators reviewed sampled theorems, definitions and files and raised 27 flags. Every flag was re-checked with the Lean kernel before it was accepted. One flag was a real soundness gap, found by the vacuity-and-misstatement judge. A capability recorded as "not installed" made a requirement fail whatever its provenance, so a record with no source could produce DOES_NOT_MEET_REQUIREMENTS. Every proof still compiled, because the spec (`ViolatesKey`) had the same hole as the code. The fix moved the trust check to the front of one line in `checkCapability` and added a `TrustedEvidence` condition to `ViolatesKey`. Three new theorems (`fail_needs_trusted`, `untrusted_never_passes`, `untrusted_never_fails`) now prove that every rejection rests on trusted evidence, and `fail_needs_trusted` is cited on every rejection's verdict card. The gap affected real data: when it was found, the catalog held 7 unsourced "not installed" records in 3 Bluefors dilution-refrigerator configurations. Applying the old rule to today's Lean outcome lattice flips 2 featured pairs (an Er:Ti:LiNbO3 quantum memory on a Bluefors LD250 or LD400) from NEED_MORE_INFORMATION to a REJECT based on no evidence. Accuracy caveat: the forensics run (commit b0b02d5) is older than the last Lean change (1b2c2dd, which added Monotone.lean with 18 theorems). So "839" describes the 9-module core as it was at audit time. The separate axiom audit is current at 48 theorems, while README, paper and VERIFICATION_SCOPE still say 44. Re-running `scripts/run_forensics.py` would bring the 839 up to date.

## paper-and-evaluation

The CryoLean paper (paper/src/paper.md, Hanah Rahman, UC Berkeley, VibeCheck FM+AI Hackathon, Nov 2026) is unfinished on main. The source stops at a literal RESULTS_PLACEHOLDER line (line 188), and neither the built PDF nor the paper/CLAIM_AUDIT.md it cites is in the repo. All evaluation numbers therefore live in paper/data/*.json (written by paper/scripts/paper_eval.py and the fig_* scripts) and in the 34 figure captions in paper/figures/meta/*.json. I recomputed the headline numbers from the JSON and they match the captions.

Every experiment ran on the paper-era catalog: 11 experiment profiles × 26 configurations = 286 pairs (Lean: 20 met, 109 not met, 157 need info). The latest catalog (site/data.json) is 204 × 220 = 44,880 pairs (3,118 met / 30,299 not met / 11,463 need info), so any slide using grid-level counts must label them as the paper evaluation.

Headline results:
- **Baselines:** a datasheet-style checker (B1) agrees with Lean on only 128/286 pairs and accepts 149 pairs Lean does not (88% of its 169 acceptances). A three-valued baseline (B2) agrees on 274 and still accepts 3.
- **Qubit-wiring demo (Demo B):** every individual spec passes, but Lean computes 14.592 µW of heat load against 12 µW of cooling power and rejects. Z3 proposes 39 flux lines, and Lean accepts that design at 11.985 µW.
- **Fault injection:** 2,750 cases, 0 of the 2,700 defective configurations accepted, and 2,750/2,750 matched an independent expected-verdict rule.
- **Z3 vs Lean:** they agree on 15,795/15,795 heat-budget designs and on 286/286 catalog pairs.
- **Gatekeeper:** it rejects sorry, native_decide, custom axioms, a false statement, and an AI-exported proof that failed with 54 compile errors.
- **Scheduling:** Lean and an independent Python checker agree on 400/400 tampered schedules.
- **Latency:** median 138 ms per verdict including process start, or 2.35 ms per pair batched, on an Intel i9-14900HX that was 35–72% busy during the benchmarks.

At least 407,836 Lean checker runs are recorded across the evaluation files.

The verified core files (Checker, Spec, Soundness, Model, Num, Schedule) have not changed since the evaluation, so the measured behaviour still describes the latest code. The proof-size numbers in the paper and e8_proofs.json (9 files, 2,578 lines, 110 theorems, 44 audited) are stale: the latest core is 10 files, 2,919 lines and 128 theorems, with 48 audited theorems, all using standard axioms only. The 839-declaration forensics audit predates Monotone.lean.

Unexpected finding: a later same-day re-run of the same 286 pairs on an updated catalog resolved 129 of the 157 undecided pairs and changed none of the 129 already-decided verdicts. That is what the proved monotonicity theorem (verdict_refines) predicts.

def trustedB : EvidenceStatus → Bool
  | .manufacturerSpecification => true
  | .experimentalObservation   => true
  | .referenceData             => true
  | .certifiedSimulation       => true
  | .userEntered               => true
  | .inferredEstimate          => false
  | .unknown                   => false
```

theorem untrusted_never_passes {k : Constraint} {c : Capability}

The site is one self-contained page, site/index.html (3,171,437 bytes); rebuilding it in memory from site/template.html plus the site/*.json files gives the committed file byte for byte, so every number below is exactly what visitors see. It has two tabs, 'Live checker' and 'Demos · 3 examples', which share three numbered panels joined by arrows: (1) choose an industry and experiment (20 industries × 3 experiments, with a 3D sample model), (2) choose a cryostat (3 featured machines per industry, shown in an illustrative three.js model), and (3) check the setup by ticking and typing each requirement, which produces verdict tiles, a two-answer box, a six-stage 'where the run fails' timeline and fixes Lean has already passed. Below the panels are a looping 'cymatic' explanation panel and a chat box. The page decides nothing itself: its instant answers are looked up from 138,564 precomputed Lean outcomes over 60 experiments × 220 machines, and replaying the page's own JavaScript logic reproduces Lean's verdict in all 13,200 of those pairs; the footer says Lean decided all 44,880 catalog pairs (204 × 220): 3,118 meet, 30,299 do not, 11,463 need information. The three Demos match the data: Mars-rover sensors on the Tinius Olsen chamber are compatible (39 of 220 machines pass); 300 mm qubit wafers on the Bluefors XLDHehp get 'need more information' only because the wafer-size need is left unticked (the documented specs would pass); and a protein crystal fails on the Lake Shore CCS-800 (no liquid nitrogen documented) but passes on the Thermo Fisher CryoMed freezer. The Demos tab adds the 180-pairing matrix (best-match machines by default: 134 met / 26 need info / 20 do not meet = 74%; original selection 113/1/66 = 63%), a 'How the physics becomes Lean' walkthrough, and a replay of the forensics audit over 839 declarations. The chat calls Claude Opus 5.5 (model id claude-opus-5-5) in cryolean/chat.py, which is told never to change Lean's verdicts. Lanyon AI only models physics when asked, and only on the full server; its first 'certified' proof was refused by CryoLean's Gatekeeper (54 Lean errors). Live Lean checks need python -m cryolean serve (FastAPI, 11 routes, Dockerfile provided); the static page answers from the precomputed outcomes, and a Vercel deploy adds the chat. On main the desktop app is a PyWebView host exposing 38 bridge methods, which shows a status page unless pointed at a frontend build; the Codex/React frontend/ that README describes is not on main (it is on the GUI_First_Pass branch). Docs and code disagree in three places: README says Lanyon answers the chat and that the catalog has 26 configurations and 11 experiments (stale); the chat box is labelled 'Chat with Lanyon AI' although Claude writes the answers; and step 6 of the physics panel still mentions a retired 'nitrogen-tank' demo. Caveat: one read-only git ls-remote showed GitHub main now at db13e1f, newer than the b2b80ff analysed here.

## product, positioning, team, links

CryoLean ("Cryo, made simple") answers one question: can this cryogenic machine physically perform my experiment under its actual operating conditions? The user is a non-expert choosing a cryostat. They pick an experiment and a machine and get one of three verdicts. Requirements met means every mandatory check passed. Does not meet requirements means a sourced equipment limit is violated, and the reason is given. Need more information means missing, untrusted or unconfirmed data, which by proof can never become a pass or a fail. Every verdict comes from a Lean 4 checker whose correctness theorems are kernel-checked (48 key theorems audited, standard axioms only). Z3, Claude and Lanyon AI only propose: Z3 gives unsat-core explanations, alternative machines, parameter fixes and one-cooldown schedules; Claude does plain-language matching, drafting and chat text; Lanyon gives repair candidates and certified simulations. Lean re-checks each proposal before the user sees it. The problem is sourced in the catalog: a Bluefors dilution refrigerator needs 24 h to reach base temperature and a DynaCool 16-40 h to start up, so a mismatch found when cold costs about a day. Today the decision is made by reading datasheets one spec at a time, which misses coupled physics: a 50-qubit wiring plan passes every spec but fails the heat budget (14.592 uW > 12 uW). Counted from the seed data, the current catalog has 220 configurations (183 equipment entries), 204 experiment profiles across 20 industries and 3,335 evidence records. Lean has decided all 44,880 pairs: 3,118 met, 30,299 not met, 11,463 need info. Several docs (README, paper, RESEARCH_SOURCES, VERIFICATION_SCOPE section 7, fig_architecture) still carry stale 26/11/667/44/121 numbers, so use the seed counts and docs/images/pipeline.svg instead. It was built for Vibecheck, "the usable formal methods hackathon" (weekend of Nov 1, 2026, TheGP, San Francisco). The event page lists no tracks or judging criteria, and three CryoLean tools come from listed sponsors (Anthropic, Lanyon AI, Forall R&D). Per git, the team is Hanah Rahman (24 commits; the paper lists UC Berkeley) and Nicholas Brandenburg (2 commits in main; his React GUI is on the unmerged GUI_First_Pass branch), plus 27 commits authored by Claude. The code is MIT-licensed at github.com/hrahman12/CryoLean and the site is live at hrahman12.github.io/CryoLean, though it is a gh-pages snapshot behind main; no Netlify or Vercel URL is recorded. Slide footers should say three things: verdicts cover the modelled requirements and sourced data, not datasheet correctness or physical certification; the public site decides nothing (verdicts are precomputed); and the 3D models are illustrative.
